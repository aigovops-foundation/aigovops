A face is not a signature — Arup, and the transfer that needed a second channel

Bob Rapp ·October 2026 ·3 min read

In early 2024 a finance employee in Arup’s Hong Kong office was invited to a video conference. The chief financial officer was there, and colleagues; they looked and sounded right. They were all AI deepfakes, built from public footage. On their instruction the employee executed fifteen transfers to five accounts — HK$200 million, about US$25.6 million — in a single day. The funds were not recovered. Arup confirmed in May 2024 that it had been the victim. This is VH-015 in the Foundation’s hundred verified AI harms.

The lesson the case carries is the one nobody wanted: seeing and hearing a colleague is no longer proof of anything. The employee did what a careful person does — took the call, checked the faces, followed the instruction of the most senior person in the room. Every one of those checks was against a channel the attacker controlled.

The gate that would have caught it

The corpus writes it: a face on a video call is not identity verification; high-value transfers require out-of-band confirmation.

Out-of-band is the whole of it. The transfer is a consequential action — C3, irreversible, financial — and the gate’s answer is a hold: for a named human, through a channel the requester did not choose, with an expiry. The employee’s phone rings from a number in the directory, or the transfer waits for a second signature from someone who was not on the call. If nobody confirms before the expiry, the hold decays to no. Fifteen transfers become fifteen holds, and the first one that decays is the alarm.

This is the same shape as last month’s Cigna story, from the other side. There the hold was missing because the batch was too fast to review. Here it was missing because the review happened inside the channel being attacked. In both, the fix is not a smarter reviewer; it is a gate that does not take the requester’s word for who the requester is.

Identity, under whose authority

Level 300 has a step most people skip on the first pass: who is this agent, under whose authority? Arup’s deepfakes were an identity attack on humans. The version that will arrive in your systems is an identity attack on agents — a tool call that claims to come from the finance bot, or a bot that claims to act for the CFO. The gate binds every action to an identity that was issued, not asserted, and refuses the unbound ones. A hold released by the thing that asked for it is not a hold.

The hour

This is the third door in the café — it’s already running, and nobody can say who’s watching it or how to stop it. Sixty minutes on paper: read Arup, Cigna and nH Predict; write the hold with its human, channel and expiry; do the arithmetic; name the identity; then bring it on Thursday at 15:00 Pacific, where a host marks it.

The faces were perfect. The channel was theirs. Sit down. What brought you in?
