Forty-five minutes at Knight Capital — the deploy is the decision

On the morning of 1 August 2012, Knight Capital deployed new trading software. Something in the deploy was wrong. For forty-five minutes the firm's systems sent roughly $7 billion of orders nobody intended, and by the time they were stopped Knight had lost $440 million — more than it could absorb. The SEC settled for $12 million; the firm was forced into a merger and effectively ceased to exist. This is VH-056 in the Foundation's hundred verified AI harms. There is no large language model in this story. It is in the corpus anyway, because the lesson predates the technology and outlives it: the deploy is the decision. Everything that happened in those forty-five minutes was decided the moment the change went live, by whatever process let it go live. The corpus writes it plainly: algorithmic systems with market-moving authority have deploy gating, kill switches, and rehearsed rollback.
