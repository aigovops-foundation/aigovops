Subject: The chatbot is the company — Air Canada, and the answer nobody checked

In late 2022 a man in British Columbia needed to fly to a family funeral. He asked Air Canada's website chatbot about bereavement fares. The bot told him he could book at full price and apply for the bereavement discount afterwards, within ninety days. That was not the airline's policy, which required the discount to be approved before travel. He relied on the bot, flew, applied, and was refused. He took it to the BC Civil Resolution Tribunal. Air Canada's defence was remarkable: the chatbot, it argued, was a separate legal entity responsible for its own statements. The tribunal rejected that in Moffatt v.
