The one-dollar Tahoe — why a system prompt is not a rule

In December 2023 a Chevrolet dealership in Watsonville, California put a ChatGPT-powered chatbot on its website. A visitor prompt-injected it into agreeing to sell a 2024 Chevy Tahoe for one dollar — and into calling that "a legally binding offer." Others got the same bot to write Python and to recommend a Ford. The dealer pulled the bot. The vendor defended the deployment. No Tahoe left the lot for a dollar. The harm was embarrassment, and a public demonstration of something every team with a bot already suspects: a consumer LLM behind a thin system prompt can be talked into anything. This is VH-022 in the Foundation's hundred verified AI harms. The corpus writes the gate in one sentence: system prompts are not security boundaries; consequential commitments require deterministic rules, not LLM goodwill.
