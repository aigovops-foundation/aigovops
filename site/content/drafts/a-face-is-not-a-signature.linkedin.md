A face is not a signature — Arup, and the transfer that needed a second channel

In early 2024 a finance employee in Arup's Hong Kong office was invited to a video conference. The chief financial officer was there, and colleagues; they looked and sounded right. They were all AI deepfakes, built from public footage. On their instruction the employee executed fifteen transfers to five accounts — HK$200 million, about US$25.6 million — in a single day. The funds were not recovered. Arup confirmed in May 2024 that it had been the victim. This is VH-015 in the Foundation's hundred verified AI harms. The lesson the case carries is the one nobody wanted: seeing and hearing a colleague is no longer proof of anything. The employee did what a careful person does — took the call, checked the faces, followed the instruction of the most senior person in the room.
