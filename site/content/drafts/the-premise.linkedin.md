The premise — Robodebt and the gate before the first notice

From 2016 the Australian government's Centrelink ran an automated scheme that took a person's annual income from tax data, averaged it across fortnights, and inferred that anyone whose earnings had been irregular had been overpaid. It then raised a debt and put the burden of disproving it on the recipient — roughly 440,000 of them. The scheme became known as Robodebt. The Federal Court settlement was about A$1.8 billion. A Royal Commission in 2023 found the scheme "neither fair nor legal," made 57 recommendations and sealed referrals; later a further ~A$475 million was added. The harm was hundreds of thousands of people pursued for debts that had been manufactured, with severe financial and psychological consequences documented in the commission's evidence. This is VH-013 in the Foundation's hundred verified AI harms.
