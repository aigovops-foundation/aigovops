The AiGovOps Library

A governance-as-code product. Members propose, stewards approve, every decision becomes a Beacon-signed receipt. Nine seats (Legal, Product Security, AI Governance, IT Security, Insurance, Safety, DevSecOps, FinOps, Public Policy) walk a real scenario through nine gates in front of a live, tamper-evident ledger. The verdicts are honest — including the ones that come back as “not yet.”

  • Identity: real GitHub OAuth. Stewards hold the gate; non-stewards see a 403 on convene.
  • Ledger: Ed25519 Beacon signatures on every accepted decision; verifiable from /status.
  • Durability: two-layer architecture — always-up CDN hub, Fly-backed dynamic core. See the durability plan.

Do real work from your phone — every send still gated

The same gate governs the whole fleet. Omni runs 30 agents with no ambient authority — the only way any of them touches the world is core.mediator.perform, and every answer lands on the ledger:

/draft Q3 governance brief
✅ draft_post — done. (verdict 1 · earned)
/publish draft-12
⏳ publish_post held for approval.
ApproveDeny

Holds fan out to your channels; an approval performs exactly once. The Jeeves Fleet cockpit shows the whole fleet at a glance — what’s pending, what’s wired, and what each agent is allowed to do — and leads with what needs you.

Vendor RFI — choose a governance stack like engineering

An open, evidence-oriented vendor RFI and audit-training tool. Learn the 2026 landscape, compose a policy-as-code stack from real options, see the honest gaps — and leave with a Beacon-style decision receipt instead of a sales deck.

  • 50 vendors, 5 categories — compliance/GRC, policy-as-code, runtime guardrails, code shipping, and OT/IIoT — side by side and filterable.
  • Neutral & gap-aware — ranking can’t be bought and gaps aren’t hidden; the OT row is honest (OT-security, not AI-model governance).
  • Audit training — lessons, a knowledge check, a self-audit, and an unsigned SHA-256 receipt you can verify.
  • Governed surface — maintained by aigovops-agent-vendor-rfi; content changes hold for steward sign-off.

One gate law runs through both.

Every action — a Library decision, an agent’s send, a vendor shortlist — is a proposal, and the gate answers in exactly one of three ways:

1

Yes — earned, with evidence. Runs once, on a single-use receipt.

0

No — refused, and written to the ledger.

?

Held for a human — and decays to 0 if no one approves by the deadline.

What “alpha” means here

Alpha means we’re shipping it openly while it’s still being shaped. You can read the code, run the demo, sign the ledger, file the bug. What you can’t do yet is treat it as production. URLs may move, schemas may change, and the steward roster is small. If something breaks or makes you say “wait, why?”, that’s a signal we want — email the team or open an issue.
