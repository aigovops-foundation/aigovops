Land acknowledgment

“We gather today in Wenatchee, on the hereditary lands of the šnp̓əšqʷáw̓šəxʷ — the P'squosa, or Wenatchi people — whose villages stood along the Wenatchee River from time immemorial, and who today live on as part of the twelve bands of the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation. We acknowledge the treaty territory of the Yakama Nation that reaches this valley, and we honor the Spokane Tribe of Indians and all fire-affected tribal communities east of us this week. We thank the tribal nations represented in this room for the use of their hereditary lands, and for their enduring care of these lands and waters.”

Wenatchee River Institute · Wenatchee Valley College

Blessing protocol — facilitator note

Invite an elder or designated representative from the tribal delegations present to open the morning with a blessing, arranged with an honorarium before the event. The blessing belongs to its giver: it is never scripted by us and never AI-drafted. If no representative is available, hold a moment of silence for fire-affected communities. Confirm specific nations represented with Expo tribal liaisons before the event.

Community-response context

The Colville Tribes run the Mount Tolman Fire Center. A unified command of tribal, state, and federal agencies demonstrates local ownership, named roles, and public receipts. Read the community / helper pages before presenting fire facts.
