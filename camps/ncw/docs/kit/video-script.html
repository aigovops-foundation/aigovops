Just west of Spokane, ponderosa pines line the river, and quiet neighborhoods climb the hills of Indian Trail and Nine Mile Falls. [pause] This is home.

On the first of August, wind-driven fire changed everything. [pause] Sixty-five thousand people left their homes. More than eight hundred buildings — gone.

When the smoke thinned, whole streets stood in ash. [pause] The largest disaster Spokane County has ever known.

And then… [pause] the helpers came.

Nearly two thousand firefighters. One hundred seventy-six jurisdictions. Two dozen states — even Australia. [pause] Tribal crews stood first attack on their own lands. A Spokane bootmaker handed firefighters new boots, right off the bench. Volunteers cooked, carried, and sheltered — families, and their pets — while neighbors drove neighbors home.

Now the rebuilding begins. And it belongs to everyone in this room. [pause] Teachers and students. Councils and small businesses. Utilities — and the tribal nations who have cared for these lands from time immemorial.

Row by row. [pause] Together.
