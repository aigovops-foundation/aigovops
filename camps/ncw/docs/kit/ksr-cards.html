Known / Suspected / Rumor

Hold up K, S, or R after each read-aloud claim. Ask: what source would move this claim into “Known”?

K

Known
Confirmed by an attributable source you can open.

S

Suspected
Credible but still incomplete, under investigation, or awaiting confirmation.

R

Rumor
Unverified, overgeneralized, or missing an openable receipt.

1. “The Old Trails Fire was started by arson.”

R — one arrest connected to one fire does not confirm a cause for Old Trails or all fires. CNN updates.

2. “Officials confirmed nothing about the causes of the other Spokane fires.”

K — that is the point of the investigation update. CNN updates.

3. “The Kaiser Canyon fire on Colville land was started by lightning.”

K — attributed coverage reports lightning. Spokesman-Review.

4. “Wind gusts above 40 mph contributed to dangerous fire conditions.”

K — a dangerous red-flag weather record is documented; read the conditions separately from ignition claims. CNN.

5. “The fires were set to clear land for data centers.”

R — no attributable receipt supports this claim.

6. “A map shared in a group chat is automatically an official evacuation map.”

R — verify zones against official alert channels; a convincing image is not a receipt. NPR.
