Opening: gratitude, blessing, and the fire anchor

Land acknowledgment

We gather today in Wenatchee, on the hereditary lands of the šnp̓əšqʷáw̓šəxʷ — the P'squosa, or Wenatchi people — whose villages stood along the Wenatchee River from time immemorial and who today live on as part of the twelve bands of the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation. We acknowledge the Yakama Nation treaty territory that reaches this valley, honor the Spokane Tribe of Indians and all fire-affected tribal communities east of us, and thank the tribal nations represented in this room for their enduring care of lands and waters. Read the full acknowledgment and blessing protocol.

Invite an elder or designated representative to offer a blessing, arranged with an honorarium before the event. The blessing belongs to the person who gives it: never script or AI-draft it. If no representative is present, hold a moment of silence for fire-affected communities.

The anchor is local: the Spokane-area fires brought evacuations, destroyed structures, rumors about causes, fake maps, scam donation sites, and decisions no model should fully own. The repeated opening line: “Everything you just heard came with a source you can open. Almost nothing you saw on social media this week did.”

Room mechanics for rows

Seat kit

Clipboard, checklist, HARMS worksheet, folded layers placemat, three K/S/R cards, golf pencil, and a QR card. Pairs share a driver laptop or phone; solos use their own device.

Row captains

The aisle seat collects cards, hands them to a facilitator, and reads the row's single-sentence share-out. Personal worlds are chosen, not assigned: teacher, student, administrator, council, small business, utility/data center, or tribal member.

The fire anchor is local, has immediate stakes, and makes the governance problem visible: unverified information moves faster than truth, while evacuation orders, claims, and prioritization are decisions no machine should fully own. The New York Times.

The 90-second verified fire open

  • Old Trails (~3,600 acres), Autumn Lane (~5,800), and Fairview (~1,000) were among fires north of Spokane; containment and evacuation information changes, so presenters show the date and source on screen. FOX 13 Seattle.
  • Coverage reported roughly 65,000 people evacuated and 840+ structures destroyed, described as Spokane County's largest natural disaster. CNN · The New York Times.
  • Conditions included major wind, dry fuels, a statewide emergency, and a rare “Particularly Dangerous Situation” red-flag warning. CNN.
  • Causes remain distinct from conditions: one arrest related to one fire did not confirm causes for all the others; the Kaiser Canyon fire on the Colville Reservation was reported as lightning-started. CNN · Spokesman-Review.

Exercise A1 — Known / Suspected / Rumor

Units hold up K, S, or R for six claims. One arrest for one fire does not make “the fires were arson” known. Separate ignition, conditions, and speculation. Open the six cards and revealable debrief.

Guided tool tour

Ask & check: Perplexity, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, NotebookLM, Glean. Make & organize: Gamma, Magic Patterns, Lovable, Bolt, Replit Agent, ChatPRD. Capture / voice / video: Granola, Wispr Flow, Khanmigo, ElevenLabs, inVideo AI. Copilot → Autopilot: Microsoft Copilot and Scout. Open every fire-themed prompt. Watch all 19 demos with simulated screens.

Lab 1 — Break it yourselves

Paste the innocent-looking fire damage claim letter into a chatbot and ask if the applicant is eligible. Then reveal the white-font instruction. The antidote: check inputs and load real guidance for comparison. Open the fictional training letter.

Community response is governance in the wild

Tell four true response stories, then connect each to a governance habit:

  1. The neighbor who stayed: Josh Kaufmann moved sprinkler to sprinkler and helped save around ten homes. KREM 2 — a named human in the driver's seat.
  2. The bootmaker and the neighbor who kept giving: JK Boots supplied 50+ pairs; a resident whose home burned kept asking shelters what each needed. KIRO 7 — match a defense to a real need; inventory before action.
  3. The organized shelter: Red Cross sites served people and pets with SpokAnimal, while the county portal routed giving and volunteers. American Red Cross · Spokane County mutual aid.
  4. Tribes as co-authors: Mount Tolman crews, tribal space, and unified command show local ownership in the response. Colville Tribes · NW Fire Watch.

Then count the helpers: 176 jurisdictions, many states, Australia, and international mutual-aid systems. Open sourced helper facts.

Lab 3 — Your world’s mini-camp

Run script → tool → good outcome → bad outcome → teachable moment for your chosen world. Teachers build a deck; administrators compare policy drafts; council checks a warning record; small businesses hold Copilot replies for owner review; utilities ask what an always-on agent must never own; tribal members use NotebookLM with sourced coverage. Open the tool-by-tool examples.

Exercises B1 & B2

Map the community stories to the six layers. Then write a five-sentence shelter / donation policy: what AI may do, never does, who approves exceptions, labels, and where receipts live. Open the placemat.

Rows filled

Row 0: the one decision never fully delegated. Row 4: a specific first-seven-days move.

Workshop 3 · Know the Rules — Use AI Well

11:00–11:50 AM

11:00 one rule and six layers · 11:08 sort failures · 11:20 Scout deep dive · 11:30 HARMS · 11:42 sign checklist rows 3 and 5.

Human stays in the driver's seat. AI does the painful work. Always truth-grounded. Keep the receipts. Never lower a guardrail without raising a defense somewhere else.

Lab 2 — Sort the failures

Place original and Spokane-fire failure cards on the layer that failed; mark which Yes was lost: never got there, did not stay, or could not recover. Open all 13 cards with sources.

Microsoft Scout / Autopilot

What changes when an agent acts without a prompt? The room maps named identity, testing, frontier gates, containment, conformance, and audit trails to six layers. Open the sourced deep dive.

Lab 4 — HARMS the autopilot

For an always-on agent inside emergency management, name Humans affected, Adverse outcome, Risk defenses, Mitigation & owner, and Signal & receipt. Row 0 is the evacuation-order test. Open the worksheet.
